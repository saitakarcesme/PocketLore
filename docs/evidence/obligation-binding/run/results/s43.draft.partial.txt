O1|P4.1,P4.2,P5.1,P5.2,P5.3,P6.1,P6.2|Apparent magnitude|material time or condition, or none|The sign and size of apparent magnitude relate to brightness such that a lower magnitude number indicates a brighter object, and negative magnitudes indicate objects brighter than the faintest stars visible to the naked eye. A difference of 1.0 in magnitude corresponds to a brightness ratio of approximately 2.512, with a magnitude 2.0 star being 2.512 times as bright as a magnitude 3.0 star, and the brightest astronomical objects having negative magnitudes like Venus at −4.2 or Sirius at −1.46. The faintest stars visible with the naked eye on a dark night have apparent magnitudes of about +6.5, while the Sun has an apparent magnitude of −26.832.
