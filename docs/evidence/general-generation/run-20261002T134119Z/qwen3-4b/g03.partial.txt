Soil covering, as described in [[S1]], involves applying a layer of material to the surface of soil to achieve benefits such as conservation of soil moisture, improvement of soil fertility and health, reduction of weed growth, and enhancement of visual appeal. Changing crops over successive seasons, as explained in [[S2]], involves growing different types of crops in the same area across multiple growing seasons to reduce reliance on specific nutrients, pest and weed pressure, and the likelihood of resistant pests and weeds.

The benefits that overlap between soil covering and changing crops over successive seasons include the reduction of weed growth and the improvement of soil health. Both practices contribute to the overall health and fertility of the soil. However, soil covering focuses on physical protection and moisture retention, while crop rotation focuses on managing nutrient balance and pest resistance.
