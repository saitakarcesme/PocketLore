Soil covering with mulch involves applying a layer of material to the soil surface, which helps in conserving soil moisture, improving soil fertility, reducing weed growth, and enhancing the visual appeal of the area [[S1]]. On the other hand, changing crops over successive seasons, known as crop rotation, involves growing different types of crops in the same area across a sequence of growing seasons to reduce reliance on specific nutrients and manage pest and weed pressure [[S2]].

The stated benefits of soil covering with mulch and changing crops through crop rotation overlap in the sense that both practices aim to improve soil health and manage weeds. However, the specific mechanisms and goals differ. Mulch focuses on moisture conservation and visual appeal, while crop rotation targets nutrient management and pest control [[S1], [S2]]. GAPS: none.
