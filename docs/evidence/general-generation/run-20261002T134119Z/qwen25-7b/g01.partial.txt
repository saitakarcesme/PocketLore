Pickling inhibits spoilage by creating an acidic and salty environment, which prevents the growth of enzymes and micro-organisms. The acidity, typically with a pH of 4.6 or lower, and the high salt content are key factors. Antimicrobial herbs and spices can also be added to enhance preservation. The process can preserve perishable foods for months or even years, depending on the specific conditions. Unlike canning, pickling does not require complete sterilization of the food, but rather relies on the acidity or salinity of the solution, the temperature of fermentation, and the exclusion of oxygen to determine the flavor and preservation effectiveness of the end product [[S1]].

In contrast, food drying removes water from food, inhibiting the growth of bacteria, yeasts, and mold. This method has been used since ancient times and involves the removal of water through evaporation using various methods such as air drying, sun drying, smoking, or wind drying. Today, electric food dehydrators or freeze-drying can be used to speed up the process and ensure consistent results [[S2]].
