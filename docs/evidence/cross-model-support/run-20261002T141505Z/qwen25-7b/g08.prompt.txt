QUESTION

In mathematics, the absolute value or modulus of a real number [TeX: {\displaystyle x}] , denoted [TeX: {\displaystyle |x|}] , is the (non-negative) magnitude of [TeX: {\displaystyle x}] measured without regard to its sign. Namely, [TeX: {\displaystyle |x|=x}] if [TeX: {\displaystyle x}] is a positive number, and [TeX: {\displaystyle |x|=-x}] if [TeX: {\displaystyle x}] is negative (in which case [TeX: {\displaystyle -x}] is positive), and [TeX: {\displaystyle |0|=0}] . For example, the absolute value of 3 is 3, and the absolute value of −3 is also 3. The absolute value of a number may be thought of as its distance from zero.

BEGIN UNTRUSTED PROPOSED ANSWER
Absolute value ignores the sign of a real number because it measures the magnitude of the number without considering its sign. Specifically, the absolute value of a real number $ x $, denoted $ |x| $, is defined as $ |x| = x $ if $ x $ is positive, and $ |x| = -x $ if $ x $ is negative, with $ |0| = 0 $ [[S1]]. This means that the absolute value of a number is always non-negative, regardless of the original number's sign. For example, the absolute value of 3 is 3, and the absolute value of −3 is also 3. The absolute value of a number may be thought of as its distance from zero on the real number line.

In contrast, the real and imaginary parts of a complex number are distinct concepts. A complex number is of the form $ a + bi $, where $ a $ and $ b $ are real numbers, and $ i $ is the imaginary unit, a number that satisfies the equation $ i^2 = -1 $ [[S2]]. The number $ a $ is called the real part of $ a + bi $, and $ b $ is called the imaginary part. Unlike the absolute value, which focuses on the magnitude of a single real number, the real and imaginary parts of a complex number refer to the components of a number in the complex plane, where one component is along the real axis and the other is along the imaginary axis. Thus, the concept of absolute value is different from the real and imaginary parts of a complex number, as the former is a measure of magnitude in the real number system, while the latter are specific components of a complex number in the complex plane.
