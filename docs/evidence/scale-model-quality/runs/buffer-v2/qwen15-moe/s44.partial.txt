Date: 2026-08-07T22:44:45Z (article revision; rendered snapshot 2026-10-01) By contributing to citizen science, amateur astronomers can contribute to science even when their primary goal is not research. They can monitor variable stars, double stars, the Sun, and occultations of stars by the Moon, as well as discovering transient astronomical events such as comets, galactic novae, or supernovae in other galaxies. Their discoveries can be valuable contributions to the field of astronomy.

O2: [S4] Subject: Apparent magnitude
Date: 2026-08-24T18:25:02Z (article revision; rendered snapshot 2026-10-01) The apparent magnitude (m) is a measure of the brightness of a star, astronomical object or other celestial objects like artificial satellites. It is a scale that ranges from negative to positive values, with the brightest objects having negative magnitudes and the faintest ones having positive magnitudes. The brightest astronomical objects have negative apparent magnitudes, such as Venus at magnitude -4.2 or Sirius at magnitude -1.46.
