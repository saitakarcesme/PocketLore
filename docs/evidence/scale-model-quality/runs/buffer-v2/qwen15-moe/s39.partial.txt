O1: Fermentation is a type of anaerobic metabolism that harnesses the redox potential of the reactants to make adenosine triphosphate (ATP) and organic end products. It is important in several areas of human society and has been used in the production and preservation of food for 13,000 years. Humans and their livestock benefit from fermentation from the microbes in the gut that release end products that are subsequently used by the host for energy. The complex metabolism utilizes a wide variety of substrates and can form nearly 300 different combinations of end products. The discovery of new fermentative organisms suggests that fermentation is more diverse than what has been studied. The end products are produced through fermentation in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes. The mild heat of pasteurization inactivates microorganisms and enzymes that contribute to food spoilage or the risk of disease, including vegetative bacteria, but most bacterial spores survive the process. Pascalization or high-pressure processing (HPP) and pulsed electric field (PEF) are non-thermal processes that are also used to pasteurize foods.
