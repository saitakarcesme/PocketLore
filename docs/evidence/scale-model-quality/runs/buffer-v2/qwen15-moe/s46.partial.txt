O1: A magnitude difference of 1 corresponds to the brightness ratio of the square root of 100, or about 2.512. A magnitude 2 star is 2.512 times as bright as a magnitude 3 star, 6.31 times as bright as a magnitude 4 star, and precisely 100 times as bright as a magnitude 7 star. The brightest astronomical objects have negative apparent magnitudes, such as Venus at magnitude -4.2 or Sirius at magnitude -1.46. The faintest stars visible with the naked eye on the darkest night have apparent magnitudes of about +6.5, though this varies depending on a person's eyesight, their altitude, and atmospheric conditions when viewing. The apparent magnitude of known objects can range from -26.832 for our Sun to +31.5 for objects in deep space imaged by the Hubble Space Telescope.
