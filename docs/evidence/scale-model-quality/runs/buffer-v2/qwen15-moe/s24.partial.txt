O1: Constitutional monarchy, also known as limited monarchy, parliamentary monarchy or democratic monarchy, is a form of monarchy in which the monarch exercises their authority in accordance with a constitution and is not alone in making decisions. Constitutional monarchy, as a form of monarchy, has a constitution that defines the principles upon which the state is based, the procedure in which laws are made, and by whom. The powers of constitutional monarchs vary, with some having virtually no executive or policy-making power and being primarily a hereditary symbolic head of state (who may be an emperor, king or queen, prince or grand duke), while in other countries, the monarch has meaningful formal powers (such as veto power, appointment power, and power to dissolve parliament).
