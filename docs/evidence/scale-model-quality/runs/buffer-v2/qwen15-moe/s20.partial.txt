O1: Constitutional monarchies typically have the same executive powers as any other form of government. In countries where the monarch exercises their authority according to a constitution, the powers are limited by established legal framework. The powers of constitutional monarchs vary, but in countries like Liechtenstein, Monaco, Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, Thailand, and Bhutan, the constitution grants substantial discretionary powers to the sovereign. In countries like the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms, the Netherlands, Spain, Luxembourg, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Lesotho, Malaysia, Cambodia, and Japan, the monarch retains significantly less, if any, personal discretion in the exercise of their authority. In countries where the monarch is a hereditary symbolic head of state, such as an emperor, king, prince, or grand duke, they typically have little executive power and are primarily a figurehead. In countries where the monarch has meaningful formal powers, such as the ability to dissolve parliament, the monarch's powers are more substantial.
