O1: A codified constitution is a written constitution that is written down into a single document or set of documents, while an uncodified constitution is written into numerous fundamental acts of a legislature, court cases, and treaties. The constitution of the United Kingdom is an example of an uncodified constitution.
O2: Constitutional monarchs can differ in power in two ways: some have significant discretionary powers, while others have limited powers and are bound to exercise powers and authorities within limits prescribed by an established legal framework. In countries like Liechtenstein, the monarch has substantial discretionary powers, while in countries like the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms, the monarch retains significantly less, if any, personal discretion in the exercise of their authority.
