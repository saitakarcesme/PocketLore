Apparent magnitude (m) is a measure of the brightness of a star, astronomical object, or other celestial objects like artificial satellites. Its value depends on its intrinsic luminosity, its distance, and any extinction of the object's light caused by interstellar dust or atmosphere along the line of sight to the observer. The brighter an object is, the lower its magnitude number. A difference of 1.0 in magnitude corresponds to the brightness ratio of [TeX: {\displaystyle {\sqrt[{5}]{100}}}] , or about 2.512. For example, a magnitude 2.0 star is 2.512 times as bright as a magnitude 3.0 star, 6.31 times as bright as a magnitude 4.0 star, and precisely 100 times as bright as a magnitude 7.0 star.
