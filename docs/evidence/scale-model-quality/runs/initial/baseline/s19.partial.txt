Constitutions are codified rather than merely written when they embody a single comprehensive document that contains the fundamental principles or established precedents that constitute the legal basis of a polity, organization, or other type of entity. This comprehensive document is often written down into a single document or set of legal documents, such as statutes, constitutions, or treaties. The constitution of the United Kingdom is an example of a codified constitution, as it is written in numerous fundamental acts of a legislature, court cases, and treaties.
